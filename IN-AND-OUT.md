# The In and Out

**Rule zero: nothing exits without a named replacement already qualified.**
A sale that leaves a hole is a gap. A sale with someone standing there is a bridge.

**Why this exists:** the sell triggers in `RULES.md` catch a collapse (ERII, revenue
−57%). They do not catch a slow bleed. BAH has lost 6.4% of its revenue in a year
while posting three straight 20%+ EPS beats — under the old rules it never trips a
wire, it just quietly shrinks for years while we congratulate it on beats.

**This list finds the exit early and starts the replacement before it's needed.**

---

## The four states

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **WATCH** | Weakening. No trigger fired. Gets re-measured monthly |
| **EXIT QUEUE** | Two or more weaknesses, or a hard trigger. **Needs a replacement named before it sells** |
| **BENCH** | Replacement qualified and waiting for the slot |
| **IN** | Bought. The bridge is crossed |

**Movement is evidence-driven, never price-driven.** "It went down" moves nothing.

---

## What puts a name on WATCH

Softer than a sell trigger — these are the early warnings the old rules missed.

| # | Warning |
|---|---|
| W1 | Revenue down year-over-year (any single year) |
| W2 | Net income down year-over-year while EPS still beats |
| W3 | Analyst consensus carries **sell** ratings |
| W4 | **Down-capture above 2.0** with no offsetting cheapness or growth |
| W5 | Insider disposals materially above the historical run-rate |
| W6 | An earnings miss over 50% |

**Two warnings = EXIT QUEUE.** One = WATCH.

### CWT — flagged, then cleared. Recording the error.

CWT was moved to EXIT QUEUE on revenue **−3.6%** and net income **−33%**. Aaron
pushed back: *"cwt bad? it's a profitable company."* He was right to.

Pulling cash flow changed the answer:

| | Operating cash flow | Capex | Net income |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2023 | $218M | $384M | $52M |
| 2024 | $291M | $471M | $190M |
| **2025** | **$303M** | **$517M** | $128M |

**Net income fell 33% while operating cash flow rose 4% — and is up 39% over two
years.** The profit drop is depreciation on a $517M build, not cash leaving. For a
regulated water utility that is the business model: build the rate base, then earn
a regulated return on it.

Real flag that remains: **long-term debt $1,105M → $1,472M (+33%)**, D/E 0.67 → 0.87.
Normal for a utility mid-build, but watch it.

**CWT → WATCH, not EXIT QUEUE.**

**The error is a repeat.** Judging a business on the wrong metric for its type:
- **DLR** — quoted P/E 93 on a REIT, where FFO is the number
- **TLN** — flagged on trailing P/E when merchant power runs on contracted cash flow
- **CWT** — net income on a capital-intensive utility, where operating cash flow is the number

**New rule: before judging any company, name which metric its business type actually
runs on.** Utilities → operating cash flow and rate base. REITs → FFO. Merchant
power → contracted cash flow. Asset-light software/chips → margins and ROE, not P/B.

## Cadence

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **Monthly** | Re-measure every holding: revenue y/y, net income y/y, down-capture, analyst mix. Move names between states |
| **On any earnings report** | Check the reporter against W1–W6 same day |
| **On any exit-queue name hitting a hard trigger** | Sell **only if** the replacement is BENCH or already owned |

**No scheduled scouting.** Most months there is no opening. A calendar that demands
a new name every day is how you end up buying a Brazilian water ADR at P/E 30
because it dropped 8% — which nearly happened on 2026-08-13.

---

## Sizing the bridge

- **Minimum new position: 3% of the book.** At $256 that's **$7.68**. Below that it cannot move anything — five existing stubs prove it
- **Cap: 25 names.** At 23 now. Past 25, adding *requires* removing
- **Exits fund entries.** Proceeds settle T+1 in a cash account — the replacement gets bought the following session, not the same day
